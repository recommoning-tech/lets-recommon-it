## What this is for

A group is about to adopt a tool: a shared server, a chat space, a tool library's booking system, a shared spreadsheet. This worksheet helps the group treat that tool as an *action situation* (Ostrom's term for a setting where people in different roles act under rules and produce outcomes) and leave with a draft charter.

It starts from the outcome, not the software. The first question is what the tool is meant to change. Everything else follows from that ([Solution First](../wiki/patterns/solution-first.md)).

[How this project is run](../governance/how-this-project-is-run.md) is this worksheet filled in for Let's Recommon IT itself.

## How to use it

- Work through it together, not alone. The people who will use the tool should be in the room.
- Each step has one main question. The sub-questions are there to help; skip any that don't apply.
- Write short answers. A sentence or two is enough. Gaps are fine; mark them and come back.
- Part 1 is about purpose and people. Part 2 is about the technology. Part 3 checks both against Ostrom's design principles. Part 4 turns the answers into charter rules.
- Each part moves through four levels of rules, from day-to-day to foundational:
  - **Operational:** what gets done, by whom.
  - **Collective-choice:** who decides the operational rules, and how.
  - **Constitutional:** how the charter itself changes.
  - **Metaconstitutional:** why any of this matters, and what it touches.

## Part 1: The tool and its purpose

### Operational level

#### 1. What resource or value is this tool meant to affect, and how?

- What is the resource (storage, money, time, knowledge, equipment, attention) or value (privacy, access, autonomy, care, trust)?
- What is the situation now, without the tool?
- What will be different when the tool is working? For whom?
- Does using the resource use it up for others (like storage or a volunteer's time), or not (like a shared document)?
- Can people be kept from using it? Should they be?
- What is this tool *not* for?

> Our answer:

#### 2. What actions are needed to make that happen?

- What do people need to do daily, weekly, yearly?
- Which actions are *using* the tool, and which are *keeping it going*?
- Which actions are allowed, required, or not allowed?
- What does someone need to know to act well, and where do they find it?

> Our answer:

#### 3. Who will do them?

- What roles are there (for example member, steward, admin, guest)?
- Who holds each role now? Is any role held by only one person? (See [Two Keyholders](../wiki/patterns/two-keyholders.md).)
- How does someone join the group or take on a role? How do they leave it?
- Is the work paid, volunteered, or rotated? Is anyone doing work nobody sees?

> Our answer:

### Collective-choice level

#### 4. Who decides the rules for steps 1 to 3, and how?

- Which decisions can one person make? Which need the group?
- How does the group decide: majority, consensus, consent, a delegated committee?
- Can the people affected by a rule take part in changing it?
- Where does discussion happen? (See [Talk Before You Enforce](../wiki/patterns/talk-before-you-enforce.md).)
- Where are decisions recorded, and does each record link to the discussion and the change it led to?

> Our answer:

#### 5. What are the costs and benefits, and who bears them?

- What does it cost in money, time, attention, and risk?
- Who pays each cost? Who gets each benefit?
- Do the people who contribute most also benefit in proportion? If not, is that a choice the group made on purpose?
- Who pays when something goes wrong?

> Our answer:

#### 6. How will we know if it is working?

- What signs would show the change from step 1 is happening?
- What can we observe or count without surveilling each other?
- Who checks, how often, and who sees the results?
- When is the first review? [date]

> Our answer:

#### 7. What will happen if it isn't working?

- At what point do we adjust, pause, or stop using the tool?
- Who can call that review?
- If we stop, how do people get their data, money, or equipment back?
- What would we use instead?

> Our answer:

#### 8. Who will monitor and enforce the rules, and how?

- Who notices when a rule is broken? Are they accountable to the group?
- What is the first response to a problem? (A conversation usually comes before anything else.)
- How do responses escalate if the problem continues?
- How are disagreements settled, quickly and cheaply?

> Our answer:

### Constitutional level

#### 9. How will this charter be changed, and by whom?

- Who can propose a change to the charter?
- How is it decided? Should it take more agreement than an operational rule?
- How often is the charter reviewed as a whole?
- Who counts as a member for charter decisions? How do new members get a voice?
- How does the arrangement end, and what happens to shared data and assets?
- Is this group part of something larger (an organization, a network, a landlord's building)? Which of its rules does the group have to follow, and which can it set for itself?

> Our answer:

### Metaconstitutional level

#### 10. Why is this important?

- What problem in the world does this address, beyond convenience?
- What values is the group drawing on?
- Why should the group govern this itself rather than leave it to a vendor or one person?

> Our answer:

#### 11. What other resources and values affect this one, and are affected by it?

- What else does the tool depend on (volunteer time, trust, other tools, electricity, funding)?
- What might it crowd out or wear down?
- Where do values pull against each other (privacy and convenience, openness and safety)?
- Who outside the group is affected?

> Our answer:

#### 12. What ethical considerations are there?

- Who could be harmed or left out?
- Does the tool concentrate power in a few hands?
- Is taking part a real choice? What happens to someone who opts out?
- Would the rules look different if the most vulnerable member wrote them?

> Our answer:

* * *

## Part 2: The technology

### Operational level

#### 13. What technologies will be used, and by whom?

- What software, hardware, hosting, and accounts are involved?
- Who uses each one: members, admins, both?
- What outside companies or services does it depend on?
- Could the group move to another tool without losing its data?
- Could a second member learn to run it in a weekend? (See [Small Enough to Understand](../wiki/patterns/small-enough-to-understand.md).)

> Our answer:

#### 14. How are users and admins given access?

- How does someone get an account? Who approves it?
- What can each role see and do? Does anyone have more access than they need?
- How many people hold full admin access? (At least two.)
- How is access removed when someone leaves?
- How does someone recover a lost password or device?

> Our answer:

#### 15. How do we make sure people can actually use it?

- How are new users shown how it works?
- What devices, languages, and connection speeds does it need to work with?
- Where do people go for help?
- Has the least technical member tried it?

> Our answer:

### Collective-choice level

#### 16. Who will develop and maintain it?

- What upkeep is needed (updates, backups, security patches, renewals)? How often?
- Who does each task? Who covers when they're away?
- How is that work recognized or paid?
- How is someone new trained to take over?

> Our answer:

#### 17. How will features be suggested and added?

- Where do people send requests?
- Who decides what gets added, and by what criteria?
- How does the group weigh a new feature against the upkeep it adds?

> Our answer:

#### 18. How will bugs be addressed?

- How does someone report a problem?
- Who sorts reports, and how quickly should people expect a response?
- What happens when the maintainers can't fix it?

> Our answer:

#### 19. How will maintainers communicate with users about changes and outages?

- What channel is used? Does it still work when the tool is down?
- How much notice is given for planned changes or downtime?
- How are unplanned outages announced, and how is the group told when they're over?

> Our answer:

### Constitutional level

#### 20. Who makes the rules about technical changes, and how are those rules updated?

- Which technical changes can maintainers make on their own?
- Which need group approval (a new vendor, a migration, a change in what data is kept)?
- Who can appoint or remove maintainers and admins?
- How do changes to these technical rules get made, and do they follow the charter process from step 9?

> Our answer:

### Metaconstitutional level

#### 21. Is it accessible?

- Can people with disabilities use it (screen readers, keyboard use, captions, contrast)?
- Does it exclude anyone by cost, device, language, bandwidth, or comfort with technology?
- Who has not been asked?

> Our answer:

#### 22. Is it ethical with data?

- What data does it collect, and is all of it needed?
- Who can see it, where is it stored, and for how long?
- Does data flow anywhere members wouldn't expect?
- Can members take their data with them, or have it deleted?

> Our answer:

#### 23. What else does the technology carry with it?

- Whose interests does the design serve? Whose does it work against?
- Does it tie the group to a company whose goals differ from the group's?
- What is its environmental cost?

> Our answer:

## Part 3: Design principles check

Elinor Ostrom found eight design principles shared by commons that lasted (*Governing the Commons*, 1990). Check each one against your answers. The goal is not a perfect score; it's to find the weakest spot and do one thing about it.

| # | Principle | Question | Steps | Yes / Partly / No | One change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear boundaries | Is it clear who can use the tool and who belongs to the group? | 1, 3, 14 | | |
| 2 | Rules fit local conditions | Do the rules and costs fit this group and this tool, and do benefits roughly match contributions? | 2, 5, 16 | | |
| 3 | Collective-choice arrangements | Can most people affected by the rules help change them? | 4, 9, 20 | | |
| 4 | Monitoring | Are the people who check on the tool and the rules members, or accountable to members? | 6, 8 | | |
| 5 | Graduated sanctions | Do responses to problems start small and escalate? | 8 | | |
| 6 | Conflict resolution | Is there a quick, low-cost way to settle disagreements? | 8 | | |
| 7 | Recognized right to organize | Can the group make its own rules without an outside party (employer, landlord, vendor, platform) overriding them? | 9, 13 | | |
| 8 | Nested enterprises | If the group is part of something larger, are decisions made at the smallest level that works, with clear links to the levels above? | 9, 20 | | |

## Part 4: From answers to charter

Turn the most important answers into rules. Sue Crawford and Elinor Ostrom's grammar of institutions gives a complete rule five parts: who it applies to (A), must, may, or must not (D), the action (I), when or where (C), and what happens otherwise (O).

> Maintainers (A) must (D) announce planned downtime in [channel] (I) at least [48 hours] in advance (C), or else the change is postponed (O).

A statement without the "or else" is a norm. Many norms work fine; just know which is which.

A charter can follow the worksheet's order:

Fill in the brackets. Under "Rules", list the key rules for that section in ADICO form; delete the "Rules" list where a section doesn't need one.

```markdown
# [Tool name] Charter

Adopted [date] by [group].

## Purpose
<!-- Step 1 -->
[Tool name] exists to [affect which resource or value] for [whom] by [how].
We will know it's working when [sign of success].
It is not for [out of scope].

## Members and roles
<!-- Steps 3 and 14 -->
Members are [who]. New members join by [how] and are approved by [role or process].
Roles:
- [Role, e.g. member]: [what they can do]. Held by [everyone with an account].
- [Role, e.g. admin]: [what they can do]. Held by [names]. There must always be at least [two].
- [Role, e.g. steward]: [what they can do]. Held by [names], for [term].
Someone leaves by [how]. Their access is removed within [time] by [role].

Rules:
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## How we use it
<!-- Steps 2 and 15 -->
Members use [tool] to [main actions]. Help and instructions are at [location].
New members are shown how it works by [role or process].

Rules:
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## How we decide
<!-- Step 4 -->
[Role] can decide [routine matters] alone.
[Larger matters] are decided by [majority / consensus / consent] of [who] at [meeting / poll / channel].
Discussion happens in [place] before any decision.

Rules:
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## Costs
<!-- Step 5 -->
[Tool] costs about [amount] per [period] in money and [hours] per [period] in time.
Money is covered by [who, how]. Time is covered by [who, how].
If something breaks, [who] pays for [what].

Rules:
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## Upkeep
<!-- Steps 16 to 19 -->
[Names or role] maintain [tool]. [Names] cover when they are away.
Updates and backups happen [how often] and are done by [who].
Feature requests go to [place]. [Who] decides on them using [criteria].
Bugs are reported at [place]. Members can expect a response within [time].
Changes and outages are announced in [channel], which works even when [tool] is down.
Planned changes get [time] notice.

Rules:
- Maintainers (A) must (D) announce planned downtime in [channel] (I) at least [48 hours] in advance (C), or else the change is postponed (O).
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## Technical changes
<!-- Step 20 -->
Maintainers can [routine technical changes] on their own.
[Changes such as a new vendor, a migration, or a change in data kept] need [approval process].
Maintainers and admins are appointed and removed by [who, how].

Rules:
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## Data and access
<!-- Steps 21 and 22 -->
[Tool] collects [what data]. It is stored at [where] for [how long].
It can be seen by [who]. It is not shared with [whom].
Members can export or delete their data by [how].
We check that [tool] works for [accessibility needs] by [how].

Rules:
- Admins (A) must not (D) read members' data (I) without the member's permission (C), or else [consequence] (O).
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## Checking in
<!-- Steps 6 to 8 -->
[Who] checks [signs of success] every [period] and reports to [whom].
Problems are raised first by [talking with the person / raising it in place].
If a problem continues, the next steps are [step 1], then [step 2], then [step 3].
Disagreements are settled by [who, how].
If [tool] isn't working, [who] can call a review. The group may adjust, pause, or stop using it.
First review: [date].

Rules:
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).

## Changing this charter
<!-- Steps 9 and 20 -->
Any [member / role] can propose a change by [how].
Changes are adopted by [rule, e.g. two-thirds of members] after [notice period].
The whole charter is reviewed every [period].
This charter must also follow [rules of any larger organization, landlord, or law].
If the group ends, members get [time] to export their data, then [what happens to data and assets].

Rules:
- [Who] (A) [must / may / must not] (D) [action] (I) [when or where] (C), or else [consequence] (O).
```

## Maintenance

- Keep the filled-in worksheet next to the charter. When someone asks why a rule exists, the answer is in the step it came from.
- Revisit the worksheet at each charter review (step 9). Start with the steps whose answers have changed.
- Run the design principles check (Part 3) again after any big change: new members, a new tool, a new maintainer.

## Community considerations

- **Who fills it in:** the people who will use the tool, not only the people who will run it. If only maintainers answer, Part 1 ends up describing the technology.
- **Who isn't in the room:** note them, and ask them about the steps that affect them most, especially 12, 15, 21, and 22.
- **Disagreement:** a step the group can't agree on is a decision for step 4, not a reason to stop. Mark it and move on.

## Patterns introduced

### [Every Page Links Out](../wiki/patterns/every-page-links-out.md)
A charter cut off from the reasons behind it turns into a list of orders, and the next group can't tell which rules still make sense. The worksheet keeps the reasons: each charter section names the steps it came from, and step 4 asks that every decision link to its discussion and the change it led to.

> **Therefore:** link every page to the other pieces in its thread, to the table, to the patterns it introduces or applies, and to the sources it draws on.

## Patterns applied

- [Solution First](../wiki/patterns/solution-first.md): step 1 asks what the tool is meant to change before anything about the tool itself.

- [Two Keyholders](../wiki/patterns/two-keyholders.md): steps 3 and 14 ask who holds each role and require at least two people with full admin access.
- [Talk Before You Enforce](../wiki/patterns/talk-before-you-enforce.md): steps 4 and 8 set up a place to talk and make a conversation the first response to a problem.
- [Small Enough to Understand](../wiki/patterns/small-enough-to-understand.md): step 13 asks whether a second member could learn to run the technology in a weekend.

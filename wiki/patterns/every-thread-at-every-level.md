## Context
Writing about technology for people who want to build and run it together, produced by many contributors over time.

## Problem
Most writing about technology stays at one level. Tutorials explain how to build something but not who decides how it's run. Critique explains what's wrong and why, but not what to build. Governance writing describes rules with nothing concrete to govern. The connections between levels, which is where the rules are, go unwritten.

## Discussion
Ostrom's IAD framework nests action situations in four levels: operational, collective-choice, constitutional, and metaconstitutional. The outcomes of each level are the rules for the level below ([*Understanding Institutional Diversity*](../../bibliography/ostrom-2005-understanding-institutional-diversity.md)). The levels map onto kinds of writing. A guide covers the operational question: how to do it. A governance piece covers collective-choice and constitutional questions: how the rules change, and who changes them. A theory essay covers the metaconstitutional question: what the resource is, who it's for, and what counts as a good outcome.

Governance holds two levels because in a small group the same people usually change the rules and decide who changes them, often in the same meeting. The pattern's `level` keeps the distinction.

Alexander paired *The Timeless Way of Building* (why) with *A Pattern Language* (what to do) ([*A Pattern Language*](../../bibliography/alexander-1977-pattern-language.md)). Bollier and Helfrich's triad of social life, peer governance, and provisioning is a similar split ([*Free, Fair, and Alive*](../../bibliography/bollier-helfrich-2019-free-fair-and-alive.md)).

## Therefore
**Write about each thread at every level: guides for operational patterns, governance for collective-choice and constitutional patterns, theory for metaconstitutional ones. Give every pattern a thread and a level, and introduce it in a piece at that level.**

*Hear what works before you hear what's wrong.*

## Context
A new or revised pattern has been proposed for a shared pattern language.

## Problem
Review by a single approver checks format and facts, but not whether the pattern makes sense to someone who wasn't there. Authors defend their drafts, reviewers fix wording, and patterns are merged without anyone finding out whether they read clearly to others.

## Forces
- Authors are closest to their own knowledge and least able to see what's missing from the page.
- The patterns community developed shepherding and writers' workshops for this, and counts them among its meta-patterns ([Iba, "Pattern Language 3.0"](../../bibliography/iba-2016-pattern-language-3.md)).
- Discussion builds cooperation that unilateral decisions don't ([*Covenants With and Without a Sword*](../../bibliography/ostrom-walker-gardner-1992-covenants-with-and-without-a-sword.md)).
- Workshops take time, and the project runs on volunteers.

## Solution
**Therefore, discuss each new pattern with at least two readers besides its author before it leaves draft, with the author listening first and the readers saying what works before what could be better.**

## Actions
- Hold the workshop at a members' meeting or in the pull request.
- One reader summarizes the pattern in their own words. Then readers say what works, then what could be improved. The author asks questions at the end.
- Optionally, an experienced writer shepherds the pattern with a few rounds of feedback before the workshop.
- Record the changes in the pull request.

## Consequences
- **Positive:** patterns read clearly to people other than their author, and the group builds a shared vocabulary.
- **Negative:** it's slower, and it needs at least three people. Until the project has them, patterns stay in draft or are workshopped with outside readers.

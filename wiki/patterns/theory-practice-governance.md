## Context
Writing about technology for people who want to build and run it together.

## Problem
Most writing about technology covers one side and leaves out the others. Critique explains what's wrong but not what to build. Tutorials explain how to build but not why, or who decides. Governance writing describes rules with nothing concrete to govern. Readers are left to connect the pieces themselves, and usually they don't.

## Discussion
Ostrom's IAD framework separates design, operational, and collective-choice questions ([*Understanding Institutional Diversity*](../../bibliography/ostrom-2005-understanding-institutional-diversity.md)). A theory essay covers the design question: what good looks like and why. A guide covers the operational question: how to do it. Governance covers collective choice: who decides, and how the rules change. Alexander paired *The Timeless Way of Building* (theory) with *A Pattern Language* (the patterns that follow from it) ([*A Pattern Language*](../../bibliography/alexander-1977-pattern-language.md)). Bollier and Helfrich's triad of social life, peer governance, and provisioning is a similar split ([*Free, Fair, and Alive*](../../bibliography/bollier-helfrich-2019-free-fair-and-alive.md)).

## Therefore
**Present every topic in three linked parts: the idea and the patterns that follow from it, a guide that builds a concrete example, and the governance processes for running it as a commons.**

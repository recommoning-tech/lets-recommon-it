*Let the group talk before anyone builds a fence.*

## Context
A group sharing a system has started to have problems: someone uses too much storage, someone installs something without asking, rules are unclear.

## Problem
The admin's instinct is to reach for technical enforcement: quotas, permissions, bans. These are quick, but they put the admin in charge of discipline and skip the group's own judgment. Members experience the rules as imposed from above.

## Forces
- Technical enforcement is fast and needs no meeting. Talking takes time.
- In experiments, letting people talk sharply increased cooperation, even with no enforcement. Sanctions worked best when combined with communication and chosen by the group ([*Covenants With and Without a Sword*](../../bibliography/ostrom-walker-gardner-1992-covenants-with-and-without-a-sword.md)).
- Lasting institutions use graduated sanctions and low-cost conflict resolution ([*Governing the Commons*](../../bibliography/ostrom-1990-governing-the-commons.md)).

## Solution
**Therefore, set up a place for the group to talk, and raise each problem there before building any enforcement tool.**

## Actions
- A chat channel or a regular check-in.
- If enforcement is needed, let the group choose it, and start with the smallest response that works.
- The admin carries out the group's decision instead of imposing their own.

## Consequences
- **Positive:** rules feel like the group's own, and many problems are settled without sanctions.
- **Negative:** responses are slower, and someone has to host the conversation. Urgent problems such as a security incident may need an admin to act first and explain afterwards.

## Context
A site, wiki, or body of documentation that grows piece by piece over time, often written by different people.

## Problem
Pages written one at a time tend to become islands. A guide loses the reasons behind it and becomes a recipe. A theory essay loses its practical consequences and becomes a slogan. Readers who arrive from a search engine land on one page and never see the rest.

## Discussion
A commons is a set of relationships, not a pile of resources ([*Understanding Knowledge as a Commons*](../../bibliography/hess-ostrom-2007-understanding-knowledge-as-a-commons.md); [*The Magna Carta Manifesto*](../../bibliography/linebaugh-2008-magna-carta-manifesto.md)). Alexander's patterns get their meaning from their links to larger and smaller patterns ([*A Pattern Language*](../../bibliography/alexander-1977-pattern-language.md)). The original wiki was built on the same idea.

Links have a cost: they must be kept current. Hand-maintained links drift as pages change. Prefer links the site can generate (backlinks, tag pages) where possible, and keep hand-written links to the ones that carry meaning.

## Therefore
**Every page links to the other pieces in its thread, to the table, to the patterns it introduces or applies, and to the sources it draws on. A page with no links out isn't finished.**

## Context
A site written collectively and organized as a table, with threads as rows and guides, governance, and theory as columns. Contributors have limited time.

## Problem
If the unit of contribution is large, such as a full set of theory, guide, and governance for one topic, only people with a lot of time can contribute. Finished work sits unpublished until the whole set is done, and the few people who can finish a set end up setting the direction for everyone.

## Discussion
Alexander argued that good places grow piecemeal, through many small acts of building and repair, not through master plans ([*The Timeless Way of Building*](../../bibliography/alexander-1979-timeless-way-of-building.md)). Eghbal describes how much of open source depends on casual contributors who make one small change and move on ([*Working in Public*](../../bibliography/eghbal-2020-working-in-public.md)). In IAD terms, this pattern is a boundary and choice rule made at the collective-choice level: it defines what counts as a contribution and who may make one ([*Understanding Institutional Diversity*](../../bibliography/ostrom-2005-understanding-institutional-diversity.md)).

Small contributions have a cost: a row can fill unevenly, with three guides and no governance. The table makes that visible. Empty cells are a to-do list, not a gate.

## Therefore
**Accept a contribution to any single cell of the table (one guide, governance piece, theory essay, or pattern) at any time, without waiting for the rest of its row.** Anyone may propose a new thread; it's added through the project's collective-choice rules.

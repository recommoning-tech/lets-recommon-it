## Summary
A guide to writing each element of a pattern in Iba's "concise deep style": short, readable statements that still carry specific meaning. For each element it gives key considerations, content forms (common sentence shapes), and checkpoints for revision, with examples from Iba Lab pattern languages.

## Why it's useful here
The most detailed source for this site's pattern format and the checklist in the pattern writing guide.

## Key ideas
- A pattern covers what to do, how, and why; not who, when, or where
- Solution: what to do and how, in the imperative, at a middle level of abstraction; no metaphors, no pattern name
- Problem: what goes wrong in the context without the solution; avoid blaming the reader
- Context: when the pattern becomes useful; drop information that applies to every pattern
- Forces, Actions, and Consequences add the causes, concrete examples, and results
- If two patterns share the same context and problem, they're probably one pattern
- Alexander: "Each pattern is a three-part rule, which expresses a relation between a certain context, a problem, and a solution" (*The Timeless Way of Building*, p. 247)

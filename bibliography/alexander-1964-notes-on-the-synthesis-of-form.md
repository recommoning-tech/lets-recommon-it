## Summary
Alexander's first book. It treats design as achieving "fit" between a form and its context. A designer lists the ways the form could fail to fit (misfits), then breaks those requirements into loosely coupled subsystems that can each be solved separately. He contrasts "unselfconscious" cultures, where forms adapt slowly through use and repair, with "selfconscious" cultures, where professional designers must handle complexity explicitly.

## Why it's useful here
- The roots of the pattern language idea. The 1971 paperback preface says the diagrams of small, independent problems matter more than the formal method. Those diagrams became patterns.
- Fit/misfit gives a way to evaluate community tech. Start from what goes wrong for actual users, not a feature list.
- Unselfconscious design parallels communities that adapt and repair their own tools instead of depending on outside experts.
- Influenced software engineering ideas of modularity and decomposition.

## Key ideas
- Design as goodness of fit between form and context
- Misfit variables and decomposition into loosely coupled subsystems
- Unselfconscious vs. selfconscious design processes
- 1971 preface: Alexander distances himself from the "design methods" movement

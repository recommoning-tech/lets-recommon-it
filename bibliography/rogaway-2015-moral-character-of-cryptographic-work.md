## Summary
A cryptographer argues that cryptography is inherently political: it rearranges power, deciding who can see and do what. After Snowden, Rogaway asks whether the field's failure to address mass surveillance is a moral failure of the field. He says yes, and calls on cryptographers to choose problems that help ordinary people resist surveillance rather than serving abstract theory or state and corporate interests.

## Why it's useful here
- Shows that technical choices carry politics, from inside a technical discipline. It's a cryptographer's version of Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?"
- Supports choosing tools for the blog's guides by whose power they protect, not just by their features.
- A short, readable bridge for technical readers who are skeptical of "politics."

## Key ideas
- Cryptography rearranges power
- Mass surveillance as the field's central moral problem
- History from wartime codebreaking to the cypherpunks
- Practical suggestions for researchers: work on problems that serve the public

## Summary
Argues that "AI" is mostly a marketing term covering unrelated technologies, used to justify replacing workers and services with unreliable automation. AI doomers and AI boosters are two sides of the same hype. The authors offer readers questions for evaluating automation claims and argue for collective resistance, especially by workers.

## Why it's useful here
- A plain-language toolkit for evaluating AI claims, which suits community audiences and complements Griziotti's denser theory.
- Supports Griziotti's point that "everything is a hallucination": LLMs produce plausible text, not knowledge.
- Bender co-wrote "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots" (2021), the paper Griziotti cites on LLM limits.

## Key ideas
- "AI" as a marketing term, not a coherent technology
- Doomers and boosters share the same hype
- LLMs as text synthesis machines without understanding
- Practical questions to ask of any automation claim

## Summary
Argues that Google, then Facebook and others, invented a new economic logic: claiming human experience as free raw material, turning it into behavioral data, and using it to predict and eventually shape behavior for sale to advertisers and others. Zuboff calls this **surveillance capitalism** and argues that it threatens individual autonomy and democracy.

## Why it's useful here
- **The standard reference** and the source of terms the public already knows.
- A useful contrast with Griziotti and McQuillan. Zuboff treats surveillance capitalism as a rogue mutation of an otherwise acceptable capitalism. The blog's other sources see it as capitalism continuing as usual. Worth being explicit about that disagreement.
- Detailed history of how ad-tech business models were built.

## Key ideas
- Behavioral surplus: data beyond what's needed to provide a service
- Prediction products and behavioral futures markets
- Instrumentarian power: shaping behavior at scale
- Critique: surveillance capitalism as a departure from "normal" capitalism (contested by others in this bibliography)

## Summary
Proposes a syntax for breaking any institutional statement into five components (ADICO). It distinguishes three kinds of institutional statement by which components they contain: shared strategies, norms, and rules.

## Why it's useful here
- A method for writing and auditing community tech governance documents: codes of conduct, acceptable use policies, admin procedures. Every rule should name who, what, when, and the consequence.
- Useful for analyzing platform terms of service: what is a rule with real sanctions vs. a norm with none.
- Close in spirit to code. Policies written in ADICO form are structured enough to compare and possibly to automate.

## Key ideas
- **ADICO:** Attributes (who), Deontic (may/must/must not), aIm (action), Conditions (when/where), Or else (sanction)
- Strategies = AIC; Norms = ADIC; Rules = ADICO
- Basis for the "institutional grammar" research program

## Summary
The fullest statement of the Institutional Analysis and Development (IAD) framework. Ostrom breaks any situation where people interact under rules into a common set of parts. That gives a shared language for comparing institutions as different as markets, firms, legislatures, and commons.

## Why it's useful here
- A toolkit for analyzing (and designing) how a shared server, network, or service is actually governed, beyond a checklist of principles.
- Rule types map directly onto community tech decisions: who can join (boundary), who can do what (position/choice), who sees logs (information).
- Levels of choice separate the day-to-day admin rules from the rules for changing rules, and from the founding charter.

## Key ideas
- **Action situation** parts: participants, positions, actions, outcomes, action-outcome linkages, information, costs/benefits
- **Rule types:** boundary, position, choice, aggregation, information, payoff, scope
- **Levels of analysis:** operational, collective-choice, constitutional
- Includes the grammar of institutions (ADICO) and revisits the design principles

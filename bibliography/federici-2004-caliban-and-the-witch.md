## Summary
Argues that the transition to capitalism depended on enclosing the commons and on disciplining women's bodies and reproductive labor, with the witch hunts as a central mechanism.

## Why it's useful here
Historical grounding for "enclosure," the opposite of recommoning. It also frames unpaid reproductive work, the parallel to the invisible maintenance labor that keeps shared infrastructure running.

## Key ideas
- Enclosure of the commons as a foundation of capitalism
- Reproductive labor as unwaged, devalued work
